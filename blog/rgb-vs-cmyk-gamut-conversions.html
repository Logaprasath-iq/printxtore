RGB vs CMYK Gamut Conversions

Written by Markus Vance

A common mistake in graphic design is sending digital RGB artwork files directly to offset print houses. Because monitors emit colored light while papers absorb colored inks, unconverted colors will shift, resulting in dull print outputs.

Understanding the RGB Color Space

RGB (Red, Green, Blue) is an additive color model used by glowing electronic screens. By mixing red, green, and blue light waves at varying intensities, monitors generate millions of vibrant color arrays, including neon greens and deep purples.

Understanding the CMYK Color Space

CMYK (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow, Key Black) is a subtractive color model used by physical printers. Printers apply translucent inks onto paper sheets, reflecting and absorbing ambient light rays to produce colors. The color gamut of CMYK is significantly smaller than RGB.

How to Convert Files Safely

Always set your design documents to CMYK mode in Adobe Illustrator or InDesign before adding colors. If converting pre-existing RGB photos, inspect color ranges using the 'Gamut Warning' feature in Photoshop. This highlights out-of-gamut colors that will shift during printing.

Recommended Export Settings

When saving final designs as print-ready PDFs, export using the PDF/X-1a:2001 standard. This profile automatically converts all color meshes to CMYK and embeds color profiles (such as Coated FOGRA39 or GRACoL 2013) to ensure printing accuracy.
