Custom Packaging: From Design to Print

Written by Daniel Carter

Creating branded product packaging is a detailed process. From designing vector layouts to folding the final printed sheet, each stage requires close cooperation between designers and press operators.

1. The Design Phase and Dielines

All packaging begins with a dieline—a 2D flat blueprint that shows the cuts, folds, and glue tabs of the box. Designers lay out their artwork over the dieline, keeping text inside safe boundaries to prevent clipping during folding.

2. Material Selection

Select materials based on product weight and shipping requirements:

  • Rigid Board: Dense, non-folding card boards used for luxury perfume and jewelry boxes.
  • Corrugated Cardboard: Fluted paper sheets providing strength, ideal for mailing boxes.
  • Paperboard: Lightweight folding cards suitable for retail cosmetics and food boxes.

3. Printing and Finishing

Once layouts are approved, high-speed offset presses print the designs. Applying finishes like spot UV coatings or metallic foil accents adds visual contrast before sheets move to post-press finishing.

4. Die-Cutting, Folding, and Assembly

Specialty die-cut presses cut out the flat boxes. Next, folding machines apply glue along tabs and press the sheets into shape. After a final quality check, the boxes are packaged and shipped.
